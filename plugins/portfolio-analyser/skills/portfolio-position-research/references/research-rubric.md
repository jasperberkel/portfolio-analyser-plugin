# Position research rubric

Use this rubric for every selected position. It defines comparable labels without pretending that unlike asset types have identical fundamentals.

## Freshness and sources

- Obtain the newest available issuer result, filing, or product/protocol facts.
- Check material news from roughly the last 90 days and use event dates, not only publication dates.
- Prefer primary sources. Use reputable secondary reporting when the primary source cannot establish a material event or when independent scrutiny is essential.
- Link every substantive current claim in the report. Label rumors, analyst opinions, and management guidance as such.
- State missing data and source conflicts explicitly.
- Date each decision-driving fact by its reporting period or event date; date market prices with observation time, currency, and venue when available. A new report timestamp does not refresh an old fact or snapshot price.
- Compare material evidence with the previous report when available. Separate a changed thesis from repeated headlines or unchanged concentration; otherwise say that no prior comparison was possible.

## Health labels

Choose one label and explain it from evidence:

- **Robust:** solid operating or structural condition with manageable identified risks.
- **Gemischt:** meaningful strengths and weaknesses; evidence does not support a clear healthy/unhealthy conclusion.
- **Angespannt:** deteriorating fundamentals, liquidity, structure, or execution with limited current counterweights.
- **Spekulativ:** outcome depends heavily on early technology, financing, regulation, commodity/crypto dynamics, or uncertain commercialization.
- **Nicht belastbar bewertbar:** current evidence is insufficient or the position type does not support a health judgment.

The label describes the instrument's current evidence, not whether it fits the user.

## Outlook

Use the requested horizon or 6–12 months by default.

- Set the tendency to **eher positiv**, **gemischt**, or **eher negativ**.
- Set confidence to **niedrig**, **mittel**, or **hoch**.
- Give the base-case reasoning and at least one upside and one downside driver.
- Treat management guidance as a claim, not a certainty.
- Avoid unsupported price targets and deterministic language.

Keep three judgments separate: **operating/protocol condition**, **valuation/price attractiveness**, and **thesis change**. A growing business is not necessarily an attractive stock at today's price. If reliable current pricing or suitable valuation evidence is unavailable, mark price attractiveness unassessed rather than implying upside or downside. This does not invalidate an independently supported concentration finding.

For each thesis give the strongest evidence-based counterargument, a reason not to change the position now, and an observable event or metric that would break the thesis. State urgency as **no new urgency**, **review at the named event**, or **prompt review warranted**, supported by the event and mechanism. Do not manufacture a trigger, prescribe trades, or turn the 6–12-month outlook into a trading deadline.

Confidence belongs to the specific claim: strong evidence about revenue or portfolio weight cannot supply missing confidence about future returns. Use lower confidence or an unassessed judgment where the decisive evidence is missing.

## Asset-specific evidence

### Stocks

Review recent revenue and earnings trajectory, margins or cash flow, liquidity and debt, guidance, competitive position, customer/product concentration, dilution, regulation, litigation, and execution. Check dated valuation against suitable history or peers when reliable; account for one-off earnings, currency, share class, and reporting-period differences. For pre-profit companies, emphasize cash runway, losses, financing needs, commercialization milestones, and dilution instead of forcing an earnings multiple.

### ETFs and ETCs

Review objective and index, assets and liquidity when available, costs, replication, tracking, concentration, country/sector/currency exposure, provider notices, and legal structure. An ETF is not “healthy” merely because its index rose. For ETCs, include collateral, issuer, counterparty, and limited-recourse risks.

### Crypto assets

Review protocol development, security incidents, network or ecosystem evidence, regulation, custody, liquidity, token economics, and major upgrades. Do not apply corporate revenue metrics unless a relevant operating entity is being analyzed. Crypto conclusions usually warrant lower confidence.

Software releases alone do not establish adoption or price potential. Seek dated evidence relevant to the thesis, such as usage, flows, liquidity, issuance, or network/security concentration, and mark unavailable evidence. Compare each held crypto asset using the same relevant dimensions; neither a larger weight nor an unrealized loss establishes which asset should be reduced first.

## Position section

For each selected position include:

1. Snapshot value, portfolio weight, and identifier.
2. Recent developments with dates and sources.
3. Health label with evidence.
4. Dated valuation/price evidence or an explicit unassessed result, separate from health.
5. Conditional outlook, claim-specific confidence, strongest counterargument, and thesis-breaking trigger.
6. What changed versus the prior report, reason against immediate change, and evidence-based urgency.

These can be compact sentences rather than six repeated subheadings. End with a cross-position synthesis covering concentration, shared risk factors, and relevant alternatives. State whether evidence supports a relative instrument preference, only a structural-risk finding, or neither. Include coverage gaps without treating unresearched alternatives as inferior.
