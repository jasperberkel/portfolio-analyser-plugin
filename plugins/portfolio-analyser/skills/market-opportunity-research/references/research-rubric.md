# Market opportunity research rubric

## Research sequence

1. **Market scan:** Establish the current macro and market backdrop with dated primary data. Look for changes in rates, inflation, regulation, capital spending, earnings, supply, inventories, adoption, or positioning that could create a catalyst or invalidate a popular narrative.
2. **Candidate screen:** Build a larger candidate set before choosing the report shortlist. Reject instruments whose thesis rests mainly on price momentum, promotional claims, poor liquidity, or unverifiable access.
3. **Deep research:** Verify each shortlisted candidate with the newest available source set and at least one source independent of the issuer when the thesis involves controversy, regulation, accounting, safety, or material execution risk.
4. **Cross-check:** Compare candidates within their own asset class. Do not rank a commodity, ETF, stock, and crypto asset using one misleading universal valuation metric.

## Freshness and evidence

- Check material developments from roughly the last 90 days and the newest available reporting period.
- Use event dates, not only article publication dates.
- Link every substantive current claim.
- Label management guidance, consensus estimates, and policy proposals as claims or forecasts.
- State when data is stale, conflicting, unavailable, or distorted by one-off items.
- Do not infer broker availability from general exchange listing. Confirm it directly or mark it unverified.

## Candidate assessment

For every candidate report:

- **Research verdict:** interesting, watch, speculative, or currently unconvincing.
- **Tendency:** rather positive, mixed, or rather negative for the chosen horizon.
- **Confidence:** low, medium, or high.
- **Risk:** low, medium, high, or very high relative to its own asset class.
- **Base case:** what must continue or change for the thesis to work.
- **Upside driver:** one or more observable catalysts.
- **Downside driver:** the main failure path.
- **Invalidation:** a concrete event or metric that would undermine the thesis.

These labels summarize research quality and conditional prospects, not expected returns or suitability.

## Asset-specific checks

### Listed stocks

Review revenue and earnings quality, margins, free cash flow, balance sheet, dilution, capital allocation, guidance, valuation relative to history and peers when reliable, competitive position, customer/product concentration, regulation, litigation, and execution. For pre-profit companies, prioritize cash runway, financing needs, milestone credibility, and dilution.

### ETFs and ETCs

Review index or objective, UCITS status where relevant, TER, assets, spread/liquidity, replication, tracking, concentration, holdings, currency exposure, securities lending, provider notices, and legal structure. For ETCs and commodity vehicles include collateral, issuer, counterparty, futures-roll, and limited-recourse risks.

### Crypto assets

Review liquidity, protocol development, security incidents, decentralization, token economics, unlocks or issuance, real usage, custody, regulation, and major upgrades. Avoid tokens whose evidence is primarily community promotion. Crypto outlooks normally warrant high risk and lower confidence.

### Commodities

Review production, inventories, demand, substitution, geopolitics, weather when relevant, project lead times, and current agency forecasts. Separate the commodity thesis from the chosen investment vehicle; futures-based products can diverge through roll yield.

## Speculative radar

A speculative-radar candidate must have:

- a falsifiable public thesis;
- sufficient liquidity for an ordinary retail investor;
- current filings, provider documents, or protocol disclosures;
- a clearly named catalyst and invalidation condition;
- an explicit high or very-high risk label.

Exclude pump-and-dump patterns, anonymous teams, unaudited claims, chronic dilution without a credible funding path, or instruments whose tradability cannot be established.

## Report structure

Use a concise ranked overview followed by one section per candidate:

1. Instrument and identifier.
2. Why it is on the list now.
3. Latest facts and news.
4. Valuation, structure, or supply/demand evidence appropriate to the asset.
5. Base, upside, and downside cases.
6. Verdict, tendency, confidence, risk, and invalidation.
7. Inline primary and independent sources.

End with shared risks, monitoring dates or metrics, rejected themes, and a statement that the report is general research rather than personalized investment advice.
